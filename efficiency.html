ThermalSkinIQ™

Efficiency, Reimagined at the Building Level

It's not just how heat is produced. It's how heat is used.

Nearly all electric resistance heaters convert electricity into heat at roughly the same efficiency at the point of use. ThermalSkinIQ's advantage lies in what happens after that electrical energy becomes heat.

Rather than concentrating intense heat at a baseboard, cable, or central mechanical appliance, ThermalSkinIQ distributes low-temperature far-infrared heat across a large active surface. In a typical primary-heating installation, heaters cover approximately 55–65% of the total floor area—the coverage needed to meet the space's BTU/therm requirements.

Cold-Climate Performance Compare Heating Systems Where the Efficiency Comes From

This broad-surface approach delivers heat more uniformly into the occupied space and its surrounding materials. As the system operates, floors, finishes, and the building envelope absorb thermal energy, quickly charging the building's thermal mass. That stored energy continues to release back into the space even after the heaters switch off, turning the home itself into a critical component of the overall heating system.

The result is not an unexplained electrical-efficiency multiplier. It is a more intelligent, whole-building heating strategy designed to reduce unnecessary runtime, distribution losses, and energy waste.

Efficiency is measured across the building, not only at the heating element.

Platform evolution

From Gen 1 carbon fibre paper to ThermalSkinIQ woven carbon fabric

The Gen 1 platform proved the whole-building concept. Its carbon-fibre-paper heaters demonstrated that low-temperature, large-area electric heating could serve as the sole primary heat source while using substantially less annual energy than the conventional systems represented in the supplied model. The next development step was not to replace that principle, but to create a more controllable and scalable electrical architecture around it.

ThermalSkinIQ makes that transition through woven carbon fabric. Unlike a comparatively uniform sheet, a plain-weave carbon fabric is an engineered two-dimensional resistor network. This allows for an engineered structured network in which current can travel predominantly between the supply and return electrodes while also redistributing laterally through neighboring yarns.

Why the woven architecture matters

More deliberate current distribution

The lateral network provides defined conductive directions and crossover nodes. Electrode placement, fabric geometry and isolation boundaries can therefore be engineered around the intended current path instead of treating the active layer as an undifferentiated random carbon sheet.

Greater potential for thermal uniformity

Lateral redistribution through the woven network can help spread current around localized variations in yarn or electrode contact. The resulting architecture provides more design control over where heat is generated.

True zoning within the heater

Full-height dielectric isolation can divide the fabric into independently controlled branches. A panel can support separate current measurement, temperature sensing and control for each zone within the heater while sharing a common return electrode.

Better engineering visibility

Each branch can be characterized through resistance, current, node-voltage and thermal mapping. This makes it possible to compare materials, identify contact or isolation problems, balance branches and validate the heater before full-voltage release.

A platform for controlled refinement

The woven fabric is not only a heating material. It becomes part of an engineered electrical topology that can be tuned through yarn orientation, electrode interfaces, zone width, return geometry and control strategy.

The whole-building strategy

Where the efficiency comes from

1. Large-area, low-temperature heating

ThermalSkinIQ spreads heat across a broad active area instead of concentrating it in a small, high-temperature element. The heater surface is limited to approximately 32°C, enabling uniform delivery at a lower local watt density. This helps reduce temperature extremes and distributes useful heat more evenly throughout the room.

2. Radiant and convective heat working together

Conventional forced-air and baseboard systems depend heavily on heating the air. Warm air rises, can stratify near the ceiling and must continually be replaced as the building loses heat. ThermalSkinIQ delivers energy through a combination of radiant and natural convective pathways from the floor level, supporting a more uniform thermal environment.

3. The building becomes part of the heating system

Some of the heat delivered by ThermalSkinIQ is absorbed by the floor assembly and surrounding building mass. Those materials can continue releasing stored thermal energy after the thermostat switches the electrical input off. Rather than treating the building only as a space that loses heat, ThermalSkinIQ allows parts of the building to participate in storing, distributing and releasing it.

4. Room-by-room control

Independent thermostats allow each heating zone to respond to its own temperature, schedule and occupancy. Rooms do not all have to be heated at the same time or to the same temperature. In the documented home, two inactive heating zones were maintained through heat transferred from adjacent areas. While specific to that home, the result illustrates how distributed heating can allow neighbouring surfaces and zones to work together.

5. No central distribution system

ThermalSkinIQ generates heat where the heat is used. The system does not require heating ducts, a boiler, circulation pumps, a combustion flue, an outdoor compressor or a refrigerant circuit. Eliminating this equipment avoids conventional distribution pathways and reduces mechanical complexity.

6. Reduced energized runtime

In controlled chamber testing under a continuously maintained cold-side load, the heaters were energized for approximately 55.15 hours during 258.81 monitored hours. This produced an audited aggregate duty cycle of approximately 21.3%, equivalent to approximately 5.11 energized hours per day across the selected intervals. The observed cycling is consistent with the floor, surrounding materials and controlled environment continuing to contribute heat after electrical shutoff; the test does not assign that persistence to any single mechanism.

A better benchmark

A more intelligent measure of efficiency

Efficiency should not be judged only at the heating element. It should be evaluated across the complete building:

  • How much energy is consumed annually
  • Where the heat is generated
  • How uniformly the heat is delivered
  • How much heat is lost during distribution
  • Whether unused spaces must be heated
  • How long comfort is maintained after shutoff
  • How much mechanical infrastructure is required

ThermalSkinIQ addresses these factors as one integrated system. It combines low-temperature full-surface heating, room-by-room control, distributed radiant comfort and interaction with the thermal mass already present in the building. The woven carbon-fabric architecture adds another layer of engineering control by allowing the active heater itself to be configured, zoned, measured and refined.
